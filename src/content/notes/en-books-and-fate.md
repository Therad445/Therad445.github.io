Macbeth is my favourite Shakespeare play, followed by Hamlet. I love their language as well as the question of what people do with circumstances they did not choose.

In Macbeth, the prophecy particularly interests me. It can be heard as a description of the future, but it can also become a reason to act. At what point does Macbeth stop waiting for fate and start arranging it?

## Returning home and facing one’s origins

I prefer Homer’s Odyssey to the Iliad, especially for the story of returning home. The episode with the name “Nobody” and the Cyclops is a favourite. Odysseus is looking for a way out of a situation that strength alone cannot solve.

The Shadow over Innsmouth, my favourite Lovecraft story, takes a very different direction. What stays with me is the attempt to escape one’s origins and where it leads. What initially appears to be an external threat turns out to be connected to the narrator himself.

I do not read everything through a single framework. But I notice that I often return to this question: what can people change, and what is already tied to their origins, past or choices? That is how an ancient epic, a tragedy and a horror story end up sharing a shelf for me.
